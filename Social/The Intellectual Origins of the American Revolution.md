# The Intellectual Origins of the American Revolution

## The Roots of Revolution

Seven Years' War allowed Britain to acquire new lands from France and Spain.
That's how the modern US borders were formed.

## The Sugar Act of 1764 Crisis

In the 18th century, the population of the colonies doubled every 25 years.
Around 50% to 80% of adult white men owned land, which allowed a middle class to emerge.

The Sugar Act of 1764 imposed a tax on sugar from the Caribbean.

## The Stamp Act of 1765 Crisis

The Stamp Act of 1765 imposed a tax on contracts.
From now on, every contract had to be signed on a paper stamped in London.
Because of a fierce colonial response, the act was repealed in 1766.

## Imperial Power, Colonial Liberty

There is a conflict in understanding the source of law:

whereas others will say that a law is unjust because it violates fundamental principles of human nature.

## Tea and Coercion

The Tea Act of 1773 makes the British East Indian Company a monopoly on tea import.
Townshend duties were applied to the tea as well.
In 1774, Coercive Acts have been enacted, which sparked the American Revolution.

## Declaring Independence

"A Common Sense" by Thomas Paine gives a critique of an ancient regime of Britain's rule.
On July 4, 1776, the Declaration of Independence is officially adopted.

## Revolutionary Constitution-Making

The American Revolution has created a bottom-up society,
opposed to a previously imposed top-down societal structure,
which was predominant in the rest of the world.

All Founding Fathers supported Republicanism,
but there all various ways a Republic can be constituted:
Thomas Paine argued strongly for a simple government,
whereas John Adams was for a complex government.
